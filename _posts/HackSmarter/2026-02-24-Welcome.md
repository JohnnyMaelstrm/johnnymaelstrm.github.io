  <h1>Anatomy of an Active Directory Compromise</h1>

  <p>In this engagement, I targeted the "Welcome" Active Directory lab by HackSmarter. This scenario simulates an "Assumed Breach" where the attacker starts with low-level phishing credentials. The objective was to enumerate the environment, identify misconfigurations, and execute a privilege escalation chain involving ACL abuse and Active Directory Certificate Services (AD CS) exploitation.</p>
  
  <p><strong>Status:</strong> Domain Compromised<br>
  <strong>Tools Used:</strong> NetExec, John the Ripper, BloodHound, BloodyAD, Certipy, Evil-WinRM</p>

  <div class="highlight-box">
    <h3>Executive Summary (The Impact)</h3>
    <p>The compromise was achieved by chaining multiple security failures:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Information Disclosure:</strong> Sensitive credentials stored in a password-protected PDF on a readable share.</li>
      <li><strong>Weak Password Policy:</strong> A valid user account was using a known default password.</li>
      <li><strong>Insecure ACLs:</strong> Users possessed <code>GenericAll</code> and <code>ForceChangePassword</code> rights over other objects.</li>
      <li><strong>AD CS Vulnerability (ESC1):</strong> A certificate template allowed low-privileged users to request Administrator certificates.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <h2>Phase 1: Reconnaissance & Enumeration</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>I started by scanning the target to identify running services. Nmap confirmed the presence of a Domain Controller with standard ports (DNS, Kerberos, LDAP, SMB) and WinRM open.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/nmap.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Nmap Scan" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Using the compromised credentials (<code>e.hills</code>), I used <strong>NetExec</strong> to enumerate SMB shares. I identified a "Human Resources" share that was readable by my user.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/smb1.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="SMB Share Discovery" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>I connected to the share using <code>smbclient</code> and downloaded all available documents for analysis.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/smb.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="SMB File Listing" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <h2>Phase 2: Information Disclosure & Cracking</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>Among the files was a "Welcome 2025 Holiday Schedule" and a "Welcome Start Guide". The Start Guide was password protected.</p>
  
  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/pdf1.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Viewing PDF" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>To access the protected content, I extracted the hash using <code>pdf2john</code> and cracked it using <strong>John the Ripper</strong>. The password was revealed to be <code>humanresources</code>.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/john.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Cracking PDF" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Upon unlocking the PDF, I discovered critical information: the default password for new employees is <code>Welcome2025!@</code>.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/protected_pdf.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Revealed Password" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <h2>Phase 3: Password Spraying & Initial Access</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>With a potential password candidate, I needed a list of valid users. I used NetExec to perform a RID brute-force to dump valid domain users.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/domain_dump_data.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="RID Brute Force" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>This gave me a clean list of targets.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/smb_users.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="User List" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Before spraying, I checked the Domain Password Policy to ensure I wouldn't lock out accounts. The policy was lenient with no strict lockout threshold.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/smb_pass_pol.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Password Policy" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>I executed the Password Spraying attack. The user <code>a.harris</code> had not changed their default password.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/smb_users_txt.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Spraying Success" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Using <strong>Evil-WinRM</strong>, I logged in as <code>a.harris</code> and retrieved the user flag.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/evil_winrm1.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="User Shell" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <h2>Phase 4: ACL Abuse (Horizontal Movement)</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>I used <strong>BloodHound</strong> to map out the Active Directory relationships.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/bloodhound_ce.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="BloodHound Ingestion" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>The analysis showed that my user <code>a.harris</code> had <strong>GenericAll</strong> rights over the user <code>i.park</code>.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/bloodhound1.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Attack Path 1" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>I also reviewed <code>a.harris</code>'s group memberships to understand their standing in the domain.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/bloodhound2.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Group Membership" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Leveraging the <code>GenericAll</code> permission, I used <strong>BloodyAD</strong> to force-change <code>i.park</code>'s password.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/bloody_ad1.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Abusing GenericAll" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <h2>Phase 5: Privilege Escalation (Vertical Movement)</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>With control over <code>i.park</code>, I checked their outbound permissions in BloodHound. This user had <strong>ForceChangePassword</strong> rights over the <code>svc_ca</code> service account.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/bloodhound3.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Attack Path 2" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>I proceeded to reset the password for <code>svc_ca</code> using <code>i.park</code>'s credentials.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/certipy3.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Resetting Service Account" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <h2>Phase 6: AD CS Exploitation (ESC1)</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>The <code>svc_ca</code> account implied the presence of Active Directory Certificate Services. I used <strong>Certipy</strong> to enumerate the available certificate templates.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/certipy1.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Enumerating Templates" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>I discovered a custom template named <code>Welcome-Template</code>. A deeper analysis confirmed it was vulnerable to <strong>ESC1</strong> (Enrollee Supplies Subject + Client Authentication).</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/certipy2.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Analyzing Template" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>The tool's vulnerability scanner confirmed the misconfiguration explicitly.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/esc1_vuln.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Vulnerability Confirmation" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Exploiting ESC1, I requested a certificate as the <strong>Administrator</strong> user, effectively forging their digital identity.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/certipy4.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Requesting Certificate" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>With the malicious <code>administrator.pfx</code> in hand, I used Certipy to perform an "UnPAC the Hash" attack to retrieve the Administrator's NTLM hash.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/certipy_hash.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Retrieving NTLM Hash" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <h2>Phase 7: Domain Dominance</h2>

  <h3>The Action</h3>
  <p>Finally, I used the captured Administrator hash to authenticate via <strong>Pass-the-Hash</strong> with Evil-WinRM, granting me full control over the Domain Controller.</p>

  <p align="center">
    <img src="{{ '/assets/Active_Directory/Welcome/evil_winrm2.png' | relative_url }}" 
         alt="Domain Admin Shell" 
         style="max-width:90%; height:auto; border:1px solid rgba(255,255,255,0.1);" />
  </p>

  <p>Mission accomplished: Reading the root flag.</p>
