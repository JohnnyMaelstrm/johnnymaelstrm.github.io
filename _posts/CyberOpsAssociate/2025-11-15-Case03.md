<p class="project-intro">
Third case for the Cyberops course. Really interesting case-type and we practised the use <span class="highlight">Security Onion</span>, <span class="highlight">Kibana</span> and <span class="highlight">Suricata</span> in a simulated incident-style investigation. We were given one <span class="highlight">pcap-file</span>.
</p>

<p>
After installing and configuring the <span class="highlight">Security Onion</span>, we downloaded the pcap onto the platform. Navigating through Alerts-view we noticed several high-severity Suricata alerts pointing to known exploit kits: <span class="highlight">RIG EK</span> and <span class="highlight">SunDown EK</span>.
</p>

<p>
We followed the timestamps and identified the initial HTTP GET request (in Wireshark) to a suspicious domain. From there, hidden iframes and JavaScript silently redirected the victim to another site hosting the exploit kits. The exploit kit probed the victim’s browser and outdated Flash plugin, eventually delivering a malicious <span class="highlight">.exe payload</span>. Shortly after the payload delivery, we observed DNS traffic for a <span class="highlight">.su domain</span> —> a top-level domain historically associated with malicious infrastructure, outbound encrypted communication to a <span class="highlight">C2(Command and Control) server</span>.
</p>

<p>
By combining <span class="highlight">Security Onion</span> alerts, <span class="highlight">Kibana</span> dashboards and additional PCAP analysis in Wireshark, we were able to piece together the entire infection flow from <span class="highlight">initial contact → exploit execution → malware download → C2 beaconing</span>. The final step was verifying file hashes through VirusTotal to confirm the presence of actual malware (<span class="highlight">SmokeLoader/Sharik</span>-type behavior).
</p>

<p>
Overall, this case was a great exercise in correlating IDS alerts with traffic patterns, understanding exploit-kit behavior, and building a coherent attack timeline from scratch.
</p>
