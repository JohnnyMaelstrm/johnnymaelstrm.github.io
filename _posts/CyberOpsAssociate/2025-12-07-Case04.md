<p class="project-intro"> Fourth case for the Cyberops course. This time we tackled a "HaiTek Ltd" scenario, investigating a full packet capture from a compromised workstation. The goal was to reconstruct the incident using <span class="highlight">Security Onion</span>, <span class="highlight">Wireshark</span> and <span class="highlight">Kibana</span>. </p>

<p> After importing the pcap, we filtered through the alerts and immediately spotted high-severity indicators for unencrypted binary downloads. We traced the traffic in Wireshark and saw the host grabbing two suspicious executables: <span class="highlight">trow.exe</span> and <span class="highlight">wp.exe</span>. This looked like a classic drive-by download or scripted drop rather than a complex exploit. </p>

<p> Almost immediately after the download, the host checked its external IP via OpenDNS and initiated a check-in with a <span class="highlight">Pushdo C2 server</span>. We also observed encrypted HTTPS traffic to a secondary IP. Since we couldn't decrypt the packet payload, we used <span class="highlight">JA3 fingerprinting</span> to identify the SSL signature. This confirmed the presence of the <span class="highlight">Gozi</span> banking trojan running alongside the botnet client. </p>

<p> A really cool find was the malware attempting to connect to a known <span class="highlight">sinkhole</span> (AnubisNetworks). The server responded with a specific cookie value ("sinkz"), essentially confirming that the threat actor's infrastructure was already being tracked by security researchers. We validated the findings by checking the file hashes in <span class="highlight">VirusTotal</span> and flagging the C2 IPs in <span class="highlight">AbuseIPDB</span>. </p>

<p> Overall, this case was excellent practice in <span class="highlight">multi-stage malware</span> analysis. </p>
